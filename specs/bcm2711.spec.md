# Broadcom BCM2711

## Orientation

The BCM2711 is the SoC of the Raspberry Pi 4 Model B, the Raspberry Pi 400 and Compute Modules 4
and 4S: four 64-bit Cortex-A72 cores beside a VideoCore VI GPU. The VideoCore and its firmware
own the boot; the Arm cores start only after the firmware has loaded a stub, a
device tree and a kernel. Peripherals sit in a 64 MB "Main peripherals" window plus an
Arm-only "ARM Local" block, and the Arm cores see them at one of two addresses depending on
whether the firmware enables Low Peripheral mode. A public datasheet (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals)
documents the registers of the blocks the Arm side may drive: interrupts, GPIO, the UARTs, SPI,
I2C, DMA, PWM, PCM and the timers. For the Ethernet MAC, PCIe and USB it gives interrupt
numbers only, and the EMMC2 controller and the GPU-controlled blocks have no chapter. This spec
rests only on documents. Facts read from the Raspberry Pi boot stub's source (timer frequency,
`SCR_EL3`, the entry exception level and the stub's patch words) are in an overlay in
`hardware-specs-permissive`; facts read from the Linux device trees (compatibles, clock names,
the CPU nodes' enable method), which are `GPL-2.0` only, are in an overlay in
`hardware-specs-gpl`. The `instances:` table is empty until IP specs for the PL011 and the
other blocks exist.

## Quick-facts

- **Addressing model.** The chip has a full 35-bit address map, seen by the Arm cores and by
  "large address" masters such as the DMA4 engines, and a 32-bit "legacy master" view seen by
  the other DMA masters. The datasheet gives peripheral addresses in the legacy view, except the
  Arm-only GIC-400 and ARM_LOCAL blocks. In the
  full map, Main peripherals are `0x4_7C00_0000`–`0x4_7FFF_FFFF` and ARM Local peripherals
  `0x4_C000_0000`–`0x4_FFFF_FFFF`. When the VideoCore enables Low Peripheral mode, the Arm cores
  alone see Main peripherals at `0x0_FC00_0000`–`0x0_FF7F_FFFF` and ARM Local at
  `0x0_FF80_0000`–`0x0_FFFF_FFFF`. Translation: legacy `0x7Enn_nnnn` is full-map
  `0x4_7Enn_nnnn` and Low Peripheral `0x0_FEnn_nnnn`, so UART0 at legacy `0x7E20_1000` is
  `0x0_FE20_1000` in Low Peripheral mode. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §1.2.1–1.2.4,
  pp. 4–6; §6.5.1–6.5.2, p. 92; §11.5, pp. 146–147)
- **Peripheral mode selection.** `arm_peri_high=1` in `config.txt` selects high peripheral mode,
  and the firmware also selects it on its own when it loads a device tree that suits it. Turning
  it on without a matching device tree stops the board booting, and the stub the firmware
  supplies does not support it, so an `armstub` of your own is needed. High peripheral mode is
  likely the one in which the Arm cores use the full-map addresses. `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi
  documentation, legacy config.txt boot options, `arm_peri_high`), `[inference]` (premises: the
  documentation names high peripheral mode as the alternative to the default `[doc]` (legacy
  config.txt boot options, `arm_peri_high`); the datasheet gives the Arm cores only two views,
  the full 35-bit map and Low Peripheral mode `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §1.2.1,
  p. 4; §1.2.3, p. 6); derivation: if high peripheral mode is not Low Peripheral mode, it is the full map;
  neither source uses both names) `TODO (verify on hardware)`: with arm_peri_high=1, read the
  GIC distributor's ID registers at the full-map address.
- **RAM.** Arm RAM starts at physical `0x0` and runs to the installed size; the VideoCore's share
  is taken from below `0x0_4000_0000` downward, sized by a `config.txt` setting. Parts carry up
  to 8 GB of LPDDR4. Installed size is not an allocation map: take usable RAM from the live
  device tree's `/memory` node(s), and exclude from general allocation every region in its
  memory reservation block and under `/reserved-memory`, unless the boot program says how a
  region may be used. A `no-map` region is left out of the OS's standard memory mapping and never
  speculatively accessed except under the control of the driver that uses it; a `reusable` one
  may hold only data its owning driver can take back. The device tree itself must not be
  overwritten while in use, reserved or not. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §1.2.3,
  p. 5), `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi documentation, BCM2711 processor page), `[standard]` (Devicetree
  Specification v0.4, §3.4 "/memory node", §3.5 "/reserved-memory", §5.3 "Memory Reservation
  Block")
- **DMA view of memory.** Legacy DMA engines must use legacy addresses: RAM through a 1 GB window
  at `0xC000_0000`–`0xFFFF_FFFF`, movable over the rest of SDRAM with the PAGE or PAGELITE bits;
  the VPU L2-cache aliases at `0x0000_0000` (allocating) and `0x8000_0000` (non-allocating)
  reach only the first 1 GB and cannot be moved. Peripherals are at `0x7C00_0000`–`0x7FFF_FFFF`
  in this view. The window is one per group, not per channel: `ENABLE.PAGE` (bits 27:24) sets it
  for DMA0–6 together and `ENABLE.PAGELITE` (bits 31:28) for the DMA Lite engines DMA7–10
  together; the DMA4 engines address the full 35-bit bus and bypass both. DMA channel 15, at
  legacy `0x7EE0_5000` apart from the others, is used only by the VPU. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM
  Peripherals, §1.2.4, p. 6; §4.2, p. 31; Table 62, p. 60; §4.6, p. 64)
- **Boot chain.** The boot ROM loads a second-stage bootloader from the SPI EEPROM; that stage
  sets up clocks and SDRAM, reads its EEPROM configuration and walks `BOOT_ORDER` (SD, network,
  USB mass storage and others) to load the VideoCore firmware, which for Raspberry Pi 4-series
  boards is the `start4*.elf` family. The firmware chooses and edits the device tree, loads the
  kernel (`kernel8.img` by default, as `arm_64bit` defaults to 1 on BCM2711 boards; 64-bit
  kernels go to `0x200000` unless `kernel_address` or `kernel_old`, which loads to `0x0`, says otherwise) and runs an Arm stub before
  it. The default stub is built into the firmware and chosen per model and settings; `armstub=` names a
  replacement file. `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi documentation, EEPROM boot flow; config.txt boot
  options, `kernel`, `arm_64bit`, `armstub`; legacy config.txt boot options, `kernel_address` and `kernel_old`;
  boot folder contents; "Device Trees, overlays, and parameters")
- **Stub load and hand-off words.** A stub file (`armstub8.bin`, or the file `armstub=` names) is
  loaded to the start of DRAM (address `0x0`) and run in AArch64 at EL3; when the stub image
  carries a magic value, the firmware writes the kernel and device-tree load addresses into
  memory words near the start of memory, where the stub reads them. `[doc]` (Trusted Firmware-A
  documentation, Raspberry Pi 4 platform, "TF-A port design") `TODO (verify on hardware)`: the
  offsets of those words; the permissive overlay gives them from the stock stub's source.
- **Kernel entry contract (Linux's requirements).** Linux asks whatever runs before it to enter
  the kernel with `x0` = the device-tree physical address and `x1`–`x3` = 0, all of DAIF masked,
  the MMU off, in non-secure EL2 (recommended) or EL1, with `CNTFRQ` programmed and `CNTVOFF`
  consistent across cores, and, on entry at EL1, `CNTHCTL_EL2.EL1PCTEN` set. It also asks for the
  device tree on an 8-byte boundary and at most 2 MB; the Image at `text_offset` above a 2 MB
  aligned base with `image_size` bytes free from the Image's start; DMA-capable devices quiesced; the Image
  cleaned to the point of coherency with no stale instruction-cache entries; every CPU in one
  coherency domain and entering at the same exception level. With EL3 present, `SCR_EL3.FIQ`
  must be the same on every CPU and unchanged while the kernel runs, and for entry at EL2
  `SCR_EL3.HCE` must be 1, both set by software at a higher exception level. These are Linux's rules, not
  observations of the stock firmware. `[standard]` (Linux arm64 boot protocol, booting.rst,
  "Setup the device tree" and "Call the kernel image", including "System registers")

  stock stub actually hands over; no Raspberry Pi document states it.
- **PSCI through TF-A.** No document describes PSCI for the stock stub. Trusted Firmware-A has a
  BL31-only port (`PLAT=rpi4`) loaded through `armstub=`: it enters the payload at AArch64 EL2
  with the device-tree address in `x0`, and patches the device tree to advertise PSCI and to
  reserve its own resident memory. The port's documentation warns it is not secure, since all its
  memory is DRAM the non-secure world can reach. `[doc]` (Trusted Firmware-A documentation,
  Raspberry Pi 4 platform)
- **SMP topology.** Four Cortex-A72 cores (Armv8-A), each with 32 kB data and 48 kB instruction
  L1 cache, sharing a 1 MB L2. In a Cortex-A72 cluster, `MPIDR_EL1` bits 1:0 hold the core
  number and bits 7:2 are zero; bits 15:8 (Aff1) and 23:16 (Aff2) come from cluster-ID inputs the
  integrator wires. Each core must set `CPUECTLR_EL1.SMPEN` (bit 6, reset 0) before enabling its
  caches and MMU or issuing cache or TLB maintenance; the bit lets the core receive instruction
  cache and TLB maintenance broadcast by other cores, and has no effect on data-cache coherence,
  which the A72 keeps regardless. `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi documentation, BCM2711 processor page),
  `[databook]` (Cortex-A72 TRM r0p3, §4.3.2, Table 4-17, pp. 4-91–4-92; §4.3.67, Table 4-79,
  p. 4-208; §5.5, p. 5-281) `TODO (verify on hardware)`: the
  cluster-ID wiring, which no document gives; read MPIDR_EL1 on each core.
- **Secondary-core release (Linux's protocol).** The live device tree names each CPU's enable
  method. For `spin-table`, the CPU's `cpu-release-addr` is a naturally aligned, zero-initialized
  64-bit word that must lie in a region reserved with `/memreserve/`, and the CPU should spin
  in reserved memory outside the kernel; the kernel writes its entry address there little-endian
  and issues `sev`, and the CPU enters with `x0`–`x3` = 0. For `psci`, the CPU stays outside the
  kernel's memory, the tree should carry a `psci` node, and the kernel brings it in with PSCI `CPU_ON`
  calls. `[standard]` (Linux arm64 boot protocol, booting.rst, CPU
  enable methods and "Secondary CPU general-purpose register settings") `TODO (verify on
  hardware)`: which method the stock firmware's device tree names; the permissive and GPL
  overlays cover the stub's spin-table and the tree's release addresses.
- **Interrupt controller.** A GIC-400 is the controller by default; `enable_gic=0` in
  `config.txt` routes interrupts through the legacy controller instead. The GIC-400 is configured
  for 4 CPUs and 192 SPIs. Its base is the Arm-only address `0x4_C004_0000`, or `0xFF84_0000` in
  Low Peripheral mode; inside the block the distributor is at `+0x1000`, the CPU interfaces at
  `+0x2000`–`+0x3FFF`, the virtual interface control at `+0x4000`–`+0x5FFF` and the virtual CPU
  interfaces at `+0x6000`–`+0x7FFF`. So in Low Peripheral mode the distributor is at
  `0xFF84_1000` and the CPU interface at `0xFF84_2000` (full map `0x4_C004_1000` and
  `0x4_C004_2000`). The GIC-400 is a GICv2 implementation with the Security Extensions; group,
  priority, acknowledge and end-of-interrupt handling follow the GICv2 architecture
  specification. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §6.1, p. 85; §6.3, p. 89; §6.5.1,
  p. 92), `[databook]` (GIC-400 TRM, §1.1, p. 1-2; §1.2, p. 1-4; §3.2, Table 3-1, p. 3-3),
  `[standard]` (IHI 0048B, chapter 3 "Interrupt Handling and Prioritization" and chapter 4
  "Programmers' Model"), `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi documentation, legacy config.txt boot options,
  `enable_gic`)
- **Interrupt IDs at the GIC.** Per core: the GIC's own virtual maintenance interrupt PPI 25,
  hypervisor timer 26, virtual timer 27, the legacy controller's FIQ 28, secure physical timer
  29, non-secure physical timer 30, the legacy controller's IRQ 31. Shared: ARM_LOCAL mailboxes
  32–47, core 0–3 PMU 48–51, AXI error 52, local timer 53, ARMC peripheral interrupts 64–79,
  VideoCore peripheral interrupts 96–159 (ID = 96 + the VC IRQ number), ETH_PCIe L2 interrupts
  160–216 (ID = 160 + the Table 103 number): AVS 169, PCIe INTA–INTD 175–178, PCIe MSI 180,
  GENET A and B 189 and 190, USB XHCI 208. PPI inputs are active-low level-sensitive; each SPI
  can be set rising-edge or active-high level. These are GIC interrupt IDs, not device-tree cell
  values. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §6.2.5, Table 103, p. 88; §6.3, Figure 7,
  p. 89), `[databook]` (GIC-400 TRM, §2.3.2, Table 2-3, and §2.3.3, p. 2-8)
- **Shared interrupt lines.** VC IRQ 57 is the OR of all five PL011 UARTs (GIC ID 153), 53 of all
  I2C controllers (149), 54 of SPI0 and SPI3–SPI6 (150), 29 of the auxiliary block's mini UART,
  SPI1 and SPI2 (125), and 62 of EMMC and EMMC2 (158); 23, 24 and 46 also pair two peripherals.
  Table 102 labels 54 "OR of all SPI", but Figure 6 routes SPI1 and SPI2 to 29. The I2C, SPI and
  UART sources behind 53, 54 and 57 are told apart in `PACTL_CS` (legacy `0x7E20_4E00`), the
  auxiliary ones in `AUX_IRQ`; for 23, 24, 46 and 62 read each peripheral's own status.
  `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §2.1, p. 8; §6.2.4, Table 102 and Figure 6,
  pp. 86–88; §6.3, Figure 7, p. 89)
- **Debug UART.** UART0 is an Arm PL011 (the datasheet names the r1p5 TRM) at legacy
  `0x7E20_1000` (`0xFE20_1000` in Low Peripheral mode); UART2–5 are further PL011s at
  `0x7E20_1400`, `…1600`, `…1800`, `…1A00`. Registers sit at 4-byte spacing with 32-bit bit
  tables (DR `0x00`, FR `0x18`, IBRD `0x24`, FBRD `0x28`, LCRH `0x2C`, CR `0x30`, IMSC `0x38`, ICR
  `0x44`). FIFOs are 32×8 transmit and 32×12 receive; IrDA is absent; only CTS/RTS of the modem
  lines exist. UART0's TXD0/RXD0 are GPIO14/15 in ALT0. Interrupt: VC IRQ 57, GIC ID 153, shared
  by all five. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §11.1, p. 144; Table 171, p. 145; §11.5,
  Table 172, pp. 146–147; Table 173, p. 148; §6.2.4, p. 87; §6.3, Figure 7, p. 89; §1.2.4,
  p. 6)
- **PL011 programming order.** Disable the UART (CR UARTEN) before changing any control register,
  then wait for the end of the character in flight. To reprogram CR, the TRM's sequence then
  flushes the transmit FIFO by clearing LCR_H.FEN, writes CR and re-enables the UART.
  IBRD and FBRD take effect only on the following LCR_H write, so write LCR_H last. TX and RX interrupts fire when the FIFO level passes through the
  trigger level, not on the level itself, so enabling the TX interrupt on an empty FIFO does not
  start interrupt-driven transmission. `[databook]` (PL011 TRM r1p5, §3.3.1, p. 3-6; §3.3.7,
  p. 3-14; §3.3.8, p. 3-16; §3.3.9, p. 3-17; §2.8.3, p. 2-23)
- **Which UART is the console.** On Raspberry Pi 4 the primary UART (GPIO14/15, header pins 8 and
  10, the Linux console) is UART1, the mini UART, and UART0 serves Bluetooth; `dtoverlay=disable-bt`
  or `miniuart-bt` makes UART0 primary; `miniuart-bt` moves Bluetooth to the mini UART and needs a
  fixed core clock (`core_freq=250` or `force_turbo=1`), and can lower Bluetooth's maximum baud
  rate. With the mini UART primary, `enable_uart` defaults to 0; `enable_uart=1` enables it and
  fixes the core clock. The page's prose says all UARTs are off by default on Compute Modules,
  but its model tables list only UART2–5 as off and give CM4 UART0 as primary, so the Compute
  Module defaults are left to the TODO. UART2–5 are off on Raspberry Pi 4, 400 and Compute
  Module 4 and 4S until an overlay enables them. UARTs are 3.3 V.

- **Early console.** Raspberry Pi's documented early console for BCM2711 boards is
  `earlycon=pl011,mmio32,0xfe201000` (UART0) or `earlycon=uart8250,mmio32,0xfe215040` (the mini
  UART), at 115200 baud by default. The firmware's initial UART0 clock is 48 MHz
  (`init_uart_clock`, UART0 only, baud at most a sixteenth of it) and its initial baud 115200
  (`init_uart_baud`). `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi documentation, UARTs, "Enable early console for
  Linux"; legacy config.txt boot options, `init_uart_clock` and `init_uart_baud`)
- **USB controllers.** On Raspberry Pi 4B and 400 the main USB ports are driven by a separate
  USB 3.0 controller attached over PCIe, not by the SoC; the SoC's legacy USB 2.0 controller
  serves the USB-C connector as a device (`otg_mode=0`, the default), and `otg_mode=1` uses an
  XHCI USB 2.0 controller as host there instead. Compute Module 4 has no USB 3.0 controller.
  Board wiring is the board specs'. `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi documentation, config.txt boot options,
  `otg_mode`)
- **Mini UART.** UART1 is the mini UART in the auxiliary block at legacy `0x7E21_5000` (shared
  with SPI1 and SPI2); its data register `AUX_MU_IO_REG` is at `+0x40`. `AUX_ENABLES` (`+0x04`)
  bit 0 gates the mini UART and all access to its registers; `AUX_MU_CNTL_REG` bits 0 and 1
  separately enable the receiver and transmitter and reset to 1, so enabling the block in its
  reset configuration starts receiving at once (a low RX line reads as a stream of `0x00`): set
  the GPIO function first. Its clock is the VPU core clock, so its baud rate moves when the core
  frequency changes unless the core clock is fixed. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §2.1,
  Table 2, p. 8; §2.1.1, Table 4, pp. 9–10; §2.2, p. 10; Table 13, p. 14), `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi
  documentation, UARTs, "Mini UART and core frequency")
- **Timers.** Each core's generic timer raises secure and non-secure physical, hypervisor and
  virtual timer interrupts (PPIs above). ARM_LOCAL `ARM_CONTROL` bit 7 drives the cores' system
  counter from the crystal (0, the reset value) or the AXI/APB clock, and bit 8 makes it count by
  one or two. The System Timer at legacy `0x7E00_3000` has a 64-bit free-running counter and four
  32-bit compare channels (VC IRQ 0–3, GIC ID 96–99). The SP804-like ARM timer's registers start
  at `+0x400` from the block base `0x7E00_B000`: LOAD `0x7E00_B400`, VALUE `…B404`, CONTROL
  `…B408`, IRQ clear `…B40C` (LOAD at `0xFE00_B400` in Low Peripheral mode); it runs from the
  variable system clock, so the datasheet advises the System Timer for accurate timing.
  `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §6.2.1, p. 86; Table 102, pp. 86–87; Figure 7, p. 89;
  §1.2.4, p. 6; §6.5.2, Table 105, pp. 92–93; §10.1, p. 142; §12.1–12.2, Table 192, p. 159),
  `[standard]` (Arm ARM DDI 0487 M.d, §D24.10.1 `CNTFRQ_EL0`, p. D24-11209)
  `TODO (verify on hardware)`: the crystal frequency, which the datasheet does not give.
  `CNTFRQ_EL0` holds only what boot software wrote and is not interpreted by hardware, so read it
  as the reported rate, read `ARM_CONTROL` for the counter's source and increment, and time the
  counter against an independent reference; the permissive overlay gives the value the stock stub
  writes.
- **ARM_LOCAL block.** Arm-only registers at `0x4_C000_0000`, or `0xFF80_0000` in Low Peripheral
  mode: the timer and AXI-error controls, per-core timer and mailbox interrupt routing, per-core
  IRQ and FIQ source registers at `+0x60` and `+0x70`, and sixteen Arm-to-Arm mailboxes
  (`MBOX_SET00` at `+0x80`). These mailboxes are not the VideoCore mailboxes in the ARMC block.
  `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §6.5.2, Table 104, pp. 92–93; §13.1–13.2, p. 163)
- **Clocks and power.** The UART clock source and dividers belong to the Clock Manager, which
  the datasheet does not describe beyond the general-purpose GPIO clock generators. The Arm side
  can query and set peripheral clocks through the VideoCore mailbox property channel (channel
  8): tag `0x00030002` returns a clock's last requested rate, `0x00030047` the measured rate after
  clamping, throttling and divider limits, and `0x00038002` sets a rate (which may be clamped);
  a rate of 0 means the clock does not exist. Clock IDs include 1 (EMMC), 2 (UART) and 12 (EMMC2);
  rates are the peripherals' base clocks, before their own dividers. Use the measured rate, not
  the requested one, to check a baud or timer calculation. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals,
  §5.4, p. 81; §11.1, p. 144), `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi firmware wiki, Mailbox property interface,
  "Clocks")
- **VideoCore mailbox transport.** The Arm reads mailbox 0 (read at `+0x00`, status at `+0x18`)
  and writes mailbox 1 (write at `+0x20`, status at `+0x38`); each word carries the channel in
  its low 4 bits and data in the upper 28. On the property channel the data is the physical
  address of a 16-byte-aligned buffer, answered in place: check the buffer's response code
  (`0x80000000` success) and each tag's response bit and length. The buffer holds its total size,
  a request code (0), the tags, a zero end tag and padding; each tag is its ID, its value-buffer
  size, a request/response word and the value, padded to 32 bits, all in the CPU's byte order.
  `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi firmware wiki, Mailboxes, "Mailbox registers"; Mailbox property
  interface, opening sections; Accessing mailboxes, "Addresses as data")

- **GPIO and pinmux.** 58 GPIOs in three banks (0–27, 28–45, 46–57), one register block at
  legacy `0x7E20_0000`: function select `GPFSEL0`–`GPFSEL5` at `0x00`–`0x14`, set/clear/level at
  `0x1C`/`0x28`/`0x34`, and pulls in `GPIO_PUP_PDN_CNTRL_REG0`–`3` at `0xE4`–`0xF0`, two bits per
  pin (00 none, 01 pull-up, 10 pull-down). The block has four interrupt lines, one per bank and
  one shared, which are VC IRQ 49–52 (GIC ID 145–148). `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals,
  §5.1, p. 65; §5.2, Table 63, p. 66; Table 90, p. 74; §6.2.4, Table 102, pp. 86–87; §6.3,
  Figure 7, p. 89)
- **Peripheral memory ordering.** The AXI system may return reads out of order when software
  moves from one peripheral to another: put a write barrier before the first write to a
  peripheral and a read barrier after the last read. An interrupt handler that reads a
  peripheral starts with a read barrier, and one that writes a peripheral ends with a write
  barrier. Once the MMU is on, map peripherals as Device memory: the A72 may over-read and merge
  accesses to Normal Non-cacheable memory, and Device memory is the type meant for
  memory-mapped peripherals. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §1.3, pp. 6–7), `[databook]`
  (Cortex-A72 TRM r0p3, §6.4.1, p. 6-291)
- **DTB runtime patching.** The firmware picks the device tree by board revision, edits it,
  applies `config.txt` parameters and overlays, and passes the kernel a pointer to the merged
  tree; it passes its own parameters in `/chosen`. Parse the live tree, not the source.
  `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi documentation, boot folder and Device Trees, "Device Trees, overlays, and
  parameters" and "Firmware parameters")

## Gotchas

- **The GIC and ARM_LOCAL bases are not legacy addresses.** Every other peripheral address in the
  datasheet is a legacy master address to translate; these two are Arm-only and translate differently
  (`0x4_C004_0000` or `0xFF84_0000` for the GIC). `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §6.5.1
  and §6.5.2, p. 92)
- **One GIC line serves many blocks.** All five PL011s raise GIC ID 153, all I2C controllers 149,
  SPI0 and SPI3–6 150, the mini UART with SPI1 and SPI2 125, EMMC and EMMC2 158. A handler reads
  `PACTL_CS` or `AUX_IRQ` where they cover the source, and otherwise each block's own status (for
  EMMC and EMMC2, always). The 57 ETH_PCIe interrupts reach the GIC individually, but the legacy
  controller sees only their OR (VC IRQ 58). `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §2.1, p. 8;
  §6.2.4–6.3, pp. 87–89)
- **Peripheral mode is a three-way agreement.** High peripheral mode without a device tree
  written for it stops the boot; and since the documentation says the stock stub lacks support
  for it, an `armstub` of your own is needed. `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi
  documentation, legacy config.txt boot options, `arm_peri_high`)
- **Pulls are set through `GPIO_PUP_PDN_CNTRL_REG0`–`3`.** They are the only pull registers in
  the GPIO register table, written directly with two bits per pin; reading them returns the
  current pull state. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §5.2, Table 63, p. 66; Table 90,
  p. 74)
- **Legacy DMA reaches 1 GB at a time.** Above the first 1 GB of SDRAM, a legacy DMA engine
  needs the PAGE (DMA0–6) or PAGELITE (DMA7–10) field moved, shared by its whole group, and the
  L2-cached aliases never reach past it. `[databook]` (BCM2711 ARM Peripherals, §1.2.4, p. 6;
  Table 62, p. 60)
- **The mini UART's baud rate follows the core clock.** Use UART0 (PL011) for a console that must
  survive frequency changes, or fix the core clock. `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi documentation, UARTs,
  "Mini UART and core frequency")
- **Not the Pi 5 chip.** The Raspberry Pi 5 family uses the BCM2712, its successor; do not assume
  any address, interrupt number or boot detail here holds for it, and use a `bcm2712` spec
  instead. `[doc]` (Raspberry Pi documentation, BCM2712 processor page)
